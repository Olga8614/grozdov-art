Photographic project

Ephemeral Art
of the Salton Sea

Olga Serebryannaya · California

The shores of California's Salton Sea are often described as abandoned, polluted, and dying. Yet beneath this reputation lies a place of extraordinary visual poetry. Along its ever-changing shoreline, wind, water, salt, sunlight, and human presence collaborate to create temporary works of art that exist for only moments, days, or seasons before disappearing forever.

The Salton Sea itself is a place in transition. As water levels recede and environmental conditions shift, familiar landmarks vanish while new textures, colors, and forms emerge. Returning to the same location weeks later rarely reveals the same scene. Every photograph therefore becomes both a celebration and a record of something that no longer exists.

Rather than focusing solely on environmental decline, this project invites viewers to discover resilience, imagination, and quiet beauty within a landscape that is often overlooked. It encourages us to slow down, observe closely, and recognize that even places facing profound ecological challenges continue to inspire wonder.

Ultimately, these images ask a simple question: How do we value beauty that cannot be preserved? The ephemeral artworks of the Salton Sea remind us that nature is constantly creating and erasing its own masterpieces. By photographing them, I hope to preserve not the objects themselves, but the fleeting moments when light, landscape, and chance briefly come together to create something extraordinary.
